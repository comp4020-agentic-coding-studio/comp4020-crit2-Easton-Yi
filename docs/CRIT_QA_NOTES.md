## 1. What organisation did you choose?

I chose the Canberra Astronomical Society, or CAS, a real community organisation established in 1969 for amateur astronomers in Canberra, the ACT and surrounding regions.

CAS brings together beginners, experienced observers and people connected with professional astronomy. It also works with the ANU Research School of Astronomy and Astrophysics at Mt Stromlo on public education and observing activities.

Official website:
https://casastronomy.org.au/

---

## 2. Why do you like this organisation?

I like that CAS makes astronomy accessible beyond the university. It connects professional research at Mt Stromlo with beginners and the wider Canberra community, and people do not need to own a telescope to begin participating.

What interests me is not only astronomy imagery, but the way CAS turns specialist knowledge into a shared public experience. Its meetings, observing activities and school outreach make astronomy feel like something people in Canberra can participate in rather than something that only happens inside research institutions.

Use this answer only if it accurately reflects my view. Do not claim previous participation that has not occurred.

---

## 3. What does CAS do?

CAS supports amateur astronomy in Canberra through activities including:

- monthly meetings;
- astronomy skills sessions;
- presentations and talks;
- members’ dark-sky observing activities;
- public stargazing;
- outreach with schools and the community;
- cooperation with professional astronomers and Mt Stromlo;
- a member publication and astronomy community.

The redesign does not need to reproduce every service or archived article. It should communicate the activities most relevant to a new visitor.

---

## 4. Who is the main audience for your redesign?

My primary audience is a Canberra resident or ANU student who is curious about astronomy but has never joined an astronomy society.

This person may not own a telescope and may not know whether CAS welcomes beginners. Their immediate questions are practical:

- Is this for someone like me?
- What can I attend?
- Where do they meet?
- What happens at a meeting?
- Do I need equipment?
- What is the safest next step?

The original website contains many of these answers, but they are distributed across different pages. My redesign brings them into a clearer visitor journey.

---

## 5. What does the current site get wrong?

The main problem is not simply that the website looks old. Its information structure reflects the content stored in the WordPress site rather than the decisions a new visitor needs to make.

The homepage mixes a welcome message, meeting updates, an external conference announcement, member images, article links and a notice about an unmaintained members’ area. These elements do not form a strong sequence.

Important beginner information is present, but it is not prominent. The statement that a visitor does not need a telescope is one of CAS’s most welcoming messages, yet a newcomer may not discover it immediately.

Information about meetings, outreach, observing, joining and location is also spread across About, Outreach, Calendar, Join and Contact pages.

Some gallery routes currently show a template error, while sidebars contain login controls, archives, tag clouds and old comments that are not useful to most public visitors.

---

## 6. Are you just criticising the site because it looks dated?

No. A dated visual style is not automatically a usability problem, and a modern appearance alone would not make my redesign better.

My critique is based on visitor tasks and information priority. The original site has valuable content, but the visitor must assemble the main story from several sections.

The redesign improves:

- orientation;
- navigation labels;
- beginner reassurance;
- activity explanations;
- distinction between public and member activities;
- access to official current information;
- responsive reading;
- accessibility;
- transparency about changing event details.

The visual redesign supports these changes rather than replacing them.

---

## 7. What is your central redesign argument?

The original site is organised around the content CAS has accumulated. My redesign is organised around what a new visitor wants to do: observe, learn, visit and join.

That argument led directly to the four-page structure:

- Home
- Observe
- Start Here
- About & Join

Every page has a defined visitor question rather than acting as a container for miscellaneous content.

---

## 8. How is your version better?

My version is better for a first-time visitor because it establishes a clear order:

1. Understand what CAS is.
2. See the kinds of activities it offers.
3. Learn that beginners are welcome.
4. Understand what a first visit may involve.
5. Continue to the official CAS calendar or joining process.

The redesign also avoids presenting stale event information as authoritative. It explains stable activity types and links visitors to official sources for changing details.

Its improvement is therefore not “more modern graphics.” It is a clearer relationship between the organisation’s values, the visitor’s questions and the next actions available.

---

## 9. What is the main design concept?

The design concept is “Canberra Night-Sky Field Guide.”

I wanted the site to feel connected to real observation in Canberra rather than resemble a generic space startup or science-fiction interface.

The visual system uses:

- deep night colours;
- warm observing-red or amber highlights;
- restrained constellation and field-guide motifs;
- clear editorial typography;
- observation-style labels for supporting information;
- a small number of meaningful astronomy images.

The atmospheric visual layer supports the content hierarchy instead of competing with it.

---

## 10. Why not just use a large star background and image gallery?

CAS is a community organisation, not only a collection of spectacular astronomy images.

A gallery-first design could repeat one of the original site’s problems by allowing imagery to dominate practical information.

The redesign uses astronomy imagery selectively to create identity, while the main experience remains focused on:

- people;
- participation;
- beginner confidence;
- location;
- observing activities;
- clear next steps.

---

## 11. Why did you make “No telescope required” so prominent?

It removes a likely barrier for the target audience.

A beginner may assume that an astronomy society is only for technically experienced people who already own expensive equipment. CAS explicitly welcomes beginners and says that owning a telescope is not required.

Making that message prominent expresses a real organisational value and helps visitors decide that the community may be accessible to them.

---

## 12. Why did you create a Start Here page?

The original site has a Join page, but joining is not necessarily the first decision a curious visitor wants to make.

“Start Here” is lower-pressure and visitor-centred. It explains:

- who is welcome;
- what may happen at a meeting;
- how to check the current calendar;
- what a prospective visitor can do;
- when the official joining process becomes relevant.

This supports the real information that prospective members may attend up to two meetings before joining.

---

## 13. Why did you combine About and Join?

For this small static redesign, organisational identity and joining are closely related.

A visitor is more likely to consider joining after understanding:

- CAS’s history;
- its connection with Mt Stromlo;
- its outreach role;
- its meeting and observing activities;
- the community benefits of membership.

Combining them keeps the navigation concise and avoids creating several thin pages.

The official joining form and payment process remain on the real CAS website.

---

## 14. Why didn’t you reproduce all the original articles and resources?

The brief requires a focused informational redesign, not a complete migration of the organisation’s production website.

Copying every article, archive and resource would:

- expand the scope without improving the main visitor journey;
- create copyright and maintenance issues;
- turn the exercise into replication rather than redesign;
- distract from the core requirements of who CAS is, what it does and how to engage.

The redesign can link back to useful official resources without copying them.

---

## 15. Did you scrape the original website or use its database?

No.

I conducted a manual content audit of relevant public pages. I extracted factual points, checked which ones mattered to the target audience, rewrote them in original language and linked back to authoritative sources.

The redesign does not connect to the original WordPress database and does not fetch CAS data at runtime.

Small repeated content structures are stored locally as TypeScript data and converted into static HTML during the Astro build.

---

## 16. How do you handle events if the site is static?

The redesign does not pretend to be a live event platform.

It explains stable activity categories, such as monthly meetings, public stargazing, outreach and members’ observing nights.

Where a visitor needs a current date, the site sends them to the official CAS calendar or outreach page.

If a specific date is included in the redesign, it must be verified against an official source and labelled with the date on which the information was checked.

This is more trustworthy than displaying stale information without context.

---

## 17. Why did you choose Astro?

Astro suits the brief because this is a small, multi-page static information site.

It provides reusable layouts and components for:

- navigation;
- page metadata;
- action cards;
- activity cards;
- location information;
- source notes;
- the footer and disclaimer.

At build time, Astro produces ordinary static HTML, CSS and images in `dist/`, which satisfies the course contract and works well with GitHub Pages.

Astro gives the project structure without adding a backend or unnecessary client-side complexity.

---

## 18. Is there any backend or full-stack functionality?

No.

There is:

- no database;
- no authentication;
- no member account;
- no booking system;
- no server-side form;
- no live calendar API;
- no payment processing.

Buttons for current events, membership and contact lead to official CAS pages.

The redesign remains an informational static site, as required by Crit 2.

---

## 19. How did you ensure the information was accurate?

I grounded the redesign in official CAS pages, including Home, About, Outreach, Calendar, Join and Contact, as well as official Mt Stromlo/ANU information where appropriate.

I recorded:

- the source URL;
- the factual point used;
- the date it was checked;
- whether the information was stable or likely to change.

For changing details, the redesign points visitors back to the official source.

I also avoided unsupported claims about equipment, access, membership fees or event availability.

---

## 20. How did you avoid copying the original content wholesale?

I separated facts from wording.

For each section, I first identified the factual meaning, then rewrote it for the specific visitor question addressed by the redesign.

For example, instead of reproducing a long membership paragraph, the redesign may present a concise visitor sequence:

- check the official calendar;
- attend as a prospective visitor;
- introduce yourself;
- decide later whether to join.

This is restructuring and rewriting rather than copying.

---

## 21. How did you select and use images?

I did not assume that all images on the CAS site were available for republication.

I prioritised public-domain or appropriately licensed Creative Commons images and recorded:

- image title;
- creator;
- source;
- licence;
- required attribution.

Images were stored locally, optimised, given useful alt text and validated as real image files.

The design uses a small number of meaningful images rather than reproducing the entire original gallery.

## 22. How did accessibility affect the design?

Accessibility was part of the design rather than a final visual check.

The implementation should include:

- semantic page landmarks;
- a logical heading hierarchy;
- visible keyboard focus;
- sufficient colour contrast;
- descriptive links;
- useful image alt text;
- reduced-motion support;
- readable line length;
- responsive layout;
- no essential meaning communicated only through colour.

The dark astronomy palette must still maintain strong text contrast.

I gave the agent a constrained project brief rather than asking it to “make a better astronomy website.”

The brief specified:

- the target audience;
- evidence-based problems in the original site;
- the redesign proposition;
- the four-page information architecture;
- required and excluded content;
- official sources;
- the visual concept;
- accessibility requirements;
- Astro’s static boundaries;
- image licensing rules;
- build and GitHub Pages requirements;

This reduced arbitrary agent decisions and made its output easier to evaluate.

## 27. What is the main limitation of the redesign?

It is a static student concept, not a production replacement for CAS’s live website.

It does not manage:

- current event data;
- member accounts;
- internal publications;
- membership payments;
- official announcements;
- organisational administration.

Its purpose is to demonstrate a clearer public-facing information architecture and design direction.

For authoritative current information, it deliberately directs visitors to CAS.

## 28. How does the project satisfy the Crit 2 brief?

The organisation is real and the redesign links to its official website.

The site communicates:

- who CAS is;
- what it does;
- where its activities are based;
- how a visitor can take a next step.

The content is selected, restructured and rewritten rather than copied wholesale.

The implementation is static and builds into `dist/`.

The improvement over the original can be articulated as a beginner-first, task-oriented information structure.

## 29. What would success look like?

A successful redesign would allow a first-time visitor to understand within a short visit:

- CAS is a Canberra amateur astronomy community;
- beginners are welcome;
- a telescope is not required;
- CAS offers meetings, observing and outreach;
- Mt Stromlo is central to its activities;
- current details should be checked through official channels;
- there is a clear, low-pressure next step.

Success is therefore measured by clarity, trust and visitor confidence, not only by visual polish.
